# Why silence is a mistake

**Conclusion.** No one may rightly silence an opinion, even one believed false.

## Mill's four-part case (chapter 2)

1. **It may be true.** To refuse a hearing is to assume our own infallibility.
2. **It may be partly true.** The received view is seldom the whole truth.
3. **Even if the received view is wholly true,** unchallenged it becomes a dead dogma, held as a prejudice.
4. **The meaning of the view itself is lost** when it is no longer contested.

The sentence from [silencing an opinion](quote-silencing.md), "He who knows only his own side of the case, knows little of that", supports step 3.

## How it connects

- It is a special case of [the harm principle](argument-harm-principle.md): silencing is coercion, and an unpopular opinion is not a harm to others.
- It shares its attitude with [Descartes's doubt](quote-cogito.md): both insist on testing a belief against its best challenge before relying on it.
