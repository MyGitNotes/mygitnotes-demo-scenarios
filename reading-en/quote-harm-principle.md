# The harm principle

The one sentence of *On Liberty* that everyone quotes. Mill states his principle in the first chapter, and the rest of the book defends it.

::::parallel-quote{author="John Stuart Mill" source="On Liberty, chapter 1" cite="Mill 1859"}
:::original
That the only purpose for which power can be rightfully exercised over any member of a civilised community, against his will, is to prevent harm to others. His own good, either physical or moral, is not a sufficient warrant.
:::
:::translation
Power may rightly be used against an adult member of a community, against his wishes, only to stop him from harming other people. That it would be good for him, in body or in character, is not enough.
:::
::::

## Reading it

- The target is *power*, whether the law or public opinion. Mill is not only talking about the state.
- "Harm to others" does the work. Offence and disapproval do not seem to count; the argument in [the harm principle as an argument](argument-harm-principle.md) depends on that.
- "His own good" is excluded. This is what makes the principle anti-paternalist.
