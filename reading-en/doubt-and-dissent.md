# Doubt and dissent

Two authors, two centuries apart, one shared habit: a belief is not yours until it has survived its strongest objection.

| | Descartes | Mill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doubt from | Inside, his own | Outside, others |
| Survives | The [cogito](quote-cogito.md) | An understood view |
| Protects | His own reasons | [Dissenters' liberty](argument-silencing.md) |

## The cogito as an argument

Descartes' side of the comparison, set out the way the Mill note does.

::::argument-map{title="The cogito" source="Descartes, *Meditations* (1641), Meditation 2; Lichtenberg, *Sudelbücher*, notebook K"}
- [C] **I exist, for as long as I am thinking**｜Med. 2

:::reason{to="C" id="R1" label="The deceiver cannot make me nothing"}
- [D] If something is deceiving me, I must exist to be deceived｜Med. 2
- [T] Doubting and being deceived are themselves ways of thinking; Descartes counts doubting, understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining and sensing as thinking｜Med. 2
:::

:::objection{to="C" id="O1" label="Lichtenberg"}
- All that is given is that there is thinking, not that there is an "I" doing it: one should say "it thinks", as one says "it flashes"｜Lichtenberg, *Sudelbücher* K 76
:::

:::objection{to="O1" id="O2" label="The certainty is local"}
- The cogito claims only that thinking guarantees existence while it lasts; Descartes adds at once that he does not yet understand what this "I" is｜Med. 2
:::
::::

## An open question

Descartes doubts alone and Mill needs other people. Can a single thinker doubt as well as a community can dissent? I lean towards no, because my doubts come from what I have already heard. That would make Mill's case stronger than he put it.
