# Silencing an opinion

From chapter 2, where Mill applies his principle to speech.

::::parallel-quote{author="John Stuart Mill" source="On Liberty, chapter 2" cite="Mill 1859"}
:::original
If all mankind minus one, were of one opinion, and only one person were of the contrary opinion, mankind would be no more justified in silencing that one person, than he, if he had the power, would be justified in silencing mankind.
:::
:::translation
Suppose everyone but one person held the same view. Everyone together would have no better right to silence that one person than he would have to silence everyone else, if he could.
:::
::::

::::parallel-quote{author="John Stuart Mill" source="On Liberty, chapter 2" cite="Mill 1859"}
:::original
He who knows only his own side of the case, knows little of that.
:::
:::translation
Someone who has heard only the case for his own view knows little even of his own side.
:::
::::

## Reading it

The first sentence says silencing is wrong even when the majority is overwhelming. The second gives one reason: you cannot hold a view well without having met its best objections. Both feed [Why silence is a mistake](argument-silencing.md).

The sentence about the single dissenter is the same kind of claim as [the harm principle](quote-harm-principle.md): a limit on what a majority may do.
