# The harm principle, as an argument

**Conclusion.** Society may coerce an adult only to prevent harm to others, never for the person's own good.

## Premises as Mill gives them

1. Coercion is itself an evil, so it needs a justification. *(Said outright in chapter 5: 'all restraint, quâ restraint, is an evil'.)*
2. The people best placed to judge what is good for a person are mostly that person. *(Chapter 4: the most ordinary person has 'means of knowledge immeasurably surpassing' anyone else's about their own circumstances.)*
3. Where a person's action harms no one else, no third party has a claim that outweighs the person's own.

Therefore, from 1 to 3: coercion over self-regarding conduct has no justification.

## The same argument as a map

The three premises support the conclusion together. The two weak points listed below the map are drawn here as objections, and Mill answers the second one.

::::argument-map{title="The harm principle" source="Mill, *On Liberty* (1859), chapters 1, 4 and 5"}
- [C] **Society may coerce an adult only to prevent harm to others, never for the person's own good**｜ch. 1

:::reason{to="C" id="R1" label="Mill's three premises"}
- [P1] Coercion is itself an evil, so it needs a justification｜ch. 5: "all restraint, quâ restraint, is an evil"
- [P2] The people best placed to judge what is good for a person are mostly that person｜ch. 4
- [P3] Where a person's action harms no one else, no third party has a claim that outweighs the person's own
:::

:::objection{to="P3" id="O1" label="What counts as harm?" status="open"}
- Mill needs "harm" narrow enough to exclude offence, but wide enough to cover real injury
:::

:::objection{to="P3" id="O2" label="Is any conduct purely self-regarding?"}
- A habit that hurts me may also hurt the people who depend on me｜ch. 4
:::

:::objection{to="O2" id="O3" label="Mill's reply"}
- Where there is "a definite damage, or a definite risk of damage" to others, the act is no longer self-regarding, and the principle does not protect it｜ch. 4
:::
::::

## Where it is weakest

- What counts as *harm*? Mill needs it narrow enough to exclude offence, but wide enough to cover real injury.
- Is any conduct purely self-regarding? A habit that hurts me may also hurt people who depend on me.
