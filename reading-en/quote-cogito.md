# Cogito

Descartes, in the second *Meditation*, has just doubted everything he can doubt: the senses, the body, even arithmetic. Something remains.

::::parallel-quote{author="René Descartes" source="Meditationes de prima philosophia, Meditation 2" cite="Descartes 1641"}
:::original
Cogitare? Hic invenio, cogitatio est, haec sola a me divelli nequit; ego sum, ego existo, certum est. Quandiu autem? nempe quandiu cogito…
:::
:::translation
Thinking? Here I find it: thinking is. This alone cannot be taken away from me. I am, I exist; that is certain. For how long? For as long as I think.
:::
::::

## Reading it

- The certainty is *local*. It covers "I exist while I think", not "I have a body" or "the world is as it seems".
- Note the question "for how long?". Descartes does not claim to survive when thinking stops, only that thinking guarantees existence.
- Compare the stance with Mill's: [Doubt and dissent](doubt-and-dissent.md).
