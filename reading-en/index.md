# Reading Mill and Descartes side by side

Notes from a short literature seminar on two old texts: John Stuart Mill's *On Liberty* (1859) and René Descartes' *Meditations on First Philosophy* (1641). Both are in the public domain. The notes keep each quotation next to its translation, then pull out the argument it carries.

## What to try

1. **Read a quotation in two languages.** [The harm principle](quote-harm-principle.md) and [Cogito](quote-cogito.md) use the `parallel-quote` directive: the original and the translation sit in one block, and a toggle hides the original.
2. **Follow the argument.** [The harm principle, as an argument](argument-harm-principle.md) and [Why silence is a mistake](argument-silencing.md) set out premises and conclusion, and link back to the quotes that support them.
3. **Check the sources.** [Bibliography](bibliography.md) uses `embed-card` blocks, one card per source, with a note on which edition I read.
4. **Follow the links.** [Doubt and dissent](doubt-and-dissent.md) joins the two authors. Open the Graph to see how the notes connect.
5. **Read the arguments as maps.** [The harm principle, as an argument](argument-harm-principle.md) and [Doubt and dissent](doubt-and-dissent.md) each hold an `argument-map`: reasons support the conclusion, objections push back, and a reply to an objection undercuts it. One objection is marked undecided.
6. **Edit freely.** This is a demo copy: your changes stay in your browser for 24 hours and never reach anyone else.
