# Lamplight Hall Film Nights: three posters that filled a village hall

**Poster series, two inks on cream stock, three weeks.** Three of four screenings sold out this season, up from none the season before.

## The problem: the cinema looked like a jumble sale

Lamplight Hall is a volunteer-run cinema in a village hall that holds 120 people. For two seasons the screenings were announced on photocopied flyers, and the people who came were the people who already knew about it. The programmer, Hedda Lund, wanted the autumn season to look like an event, with no budget for photographs of film stills (the rights alone were out of reach).

## My role

I was the only designer. I did the concept, the artwork and the print files, and I worked directly with Hedda and with Joss, the volunteer who runs the local print co-op. I did not choose the films or write the copy.

## The process

### We drew nine ideas before we picked one

I made nine small thumbnails in an afternoon, all around the same few things: a lantern, empty chairs, a projector beam, a moon. Hedda circled the one with the lantern and the lone empty chair. We then multiplied the chair into rows.

### Two inks on cream decided the look

The co-op prints on a risograph, which means one ink per pass and a slight wobble between layers. Rather than fight that, I chose **indigo and orange inks on cream stock** and designed for the wobble. Two inks also keep the whole run under the budget.

### One poster per kind of night

The season had four nights of three kinds: an opening night, two double bills and a closing night. Each kind got its own picture, and they hang as a set. The opening night is the lantern over the empty chairs at the top of this page: the hall waiting for its audience.

## What I rejected, and why

- **Film stills.** Rights and cost. Also, stills make a cinema look like a streaming page.
- **One poster for the whole season.** It would have had to list six films and ended up as the flyer again.
- **A third ink.** A third pass would have pushed the print bill past what Hedda could cover from ticket sales.
