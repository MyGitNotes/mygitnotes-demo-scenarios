# How I work

Five steps, and what you will have in hand at the end of each. The names of the steps matter less than the fact that you always know which one we are in.

## 1. Call and quote (week 0)

A thirty-minute call about what you need and what it is for. A fixed quote follows within three working days, with what is included, what is not and how many rounds of changes are in the fee.

**You have:** a quote you can say yes or no to.

## 2. Look before drawing (week 1)

I read what you have, visit the place if there is one and talk to two or three of the people who will use the result. For print jobs I also talk to the printer, since their machine decides half the design.

**You have:** a one-page summary of what I found and the direction I propose.

## 3. First ideas (week 2)

Two or three directions, drawn simply and shown together, with my recommendation and the reason for it. This is when to say "that one" or "none of these".

**You have:** directions to choose between, each with a short note on what it gives up.

## 4. Refine (weeks 3 to 4)

One direction only. Two rounds of changes are in the fee. I number every comment I receive and answer each by number, so we both know what was decided.

**You have:** the full design, tested where it will actually be used.

## 5. Hand over (week 5)

Print-ready or developer-ready files, a short guide on how to use them, and a call to walk through it. I stay available for questions for a month after.

**You have:** everything, in formats other people can open without me.

## What I need from you

One person who can decide. A single list of feedback per round instead of several. And the printer's or developer's contact early.

## What I will not do

Promise a result I cannot measure, rush a round so it ends early, or hide a trade-off. If the budget does not cover a good answer, I would rather say so on the first call.
