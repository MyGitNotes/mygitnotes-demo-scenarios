# Brief: Mossgate Tea

Signed off by Dana Okoye (owner) on the kickoff call. Everything below is what I wrote down while she talked, tidied the same evening.

## The client

Mossgate Tea is a loose-leaf tea shop with a six-seat tasting counter, opening in a former bookbinder's workshop in November. Dana buys directly from three small growers and blends two house teas. She has a shop lease, a name and nothing else: no logo, no packaging, no website.

## What she asked for

An identity that can live on four things she has already budgeted:

1. A **tin** for loose tea (three sizes, one label template).
2. A **hanging sign** over the door and a window vinyl.
3. A simple **ordering app** for regulars, built by a freelance developer.
4. A **poster** for the monthly tasting night.

## Goals

- A neighbour walking past at dusk should recognise the shop from the sign alone, without reading it.
- The tin should look right on a shelf at home, next to the good mugs, not like a "gift set".
- Nothing should say "wellness" or "ceremony". Dana's customers are people who want a decent cup, not a lifestyle.

## Constraints

| Item | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | Fixed fee, two rounds of changes after the first presentation |
| Print | Tin labels printed in two spot colours on kraft stock; sign painted by a local signwriter |
| Timeline | Presentation in week 2, final files in week 3, shop opens in week 5 |
| Existing assets | The name "Mossgate" (from the street), the arched doorway, nothing else |

## Not in scope

Website design, photography, naming of individual teas, and any food packaging beyond the tin.

## Open questions I took away

- Does Dana want the name set in capitals? (She had no opinion. I will decide and explain.)
- Can the signwriter paint a thin line? (Ask before I design one.)
