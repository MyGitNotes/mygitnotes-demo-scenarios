# Round 1: three directions

Three marks, drawn as simply as I could, each answering the research differently. All on white so nothing but the shape is judged.

## A. Gate and leaf

![Mark A: a thick rounded arch with a single leaf inside, in moss green](assets/mark-a-gate-leaf.webp)

The arched door, with a leaf standing in the opening. It comes straight from the [doorway](moodboard.md) and costs the client nothing to explain.

## B. Fern seal

![Mark B: a thin circle holding a single fern frond, in moss green](assets/mark-b-fern-seal.webp)

A round seal with a fern frond. Made for stamps, lids and bag closures, and the most "botanical" of the three.

## C. Two leaves

![Mark C: two overlapping leaf shapes, one moss green and one terracotta, leaving an arched gap](assets/mark-c-two-leaves.webp)

Two leaves overlap and the gap between them is the arch. The only one that uses two colours and the only one that hides its idea.

## Internal critique

An hour with Lena, who designs for a living but has never seen this project. I asked for a **direction review** (is this the right idea?), not a craft review (is the curve right?), so we would not argue about kerning on day four. She read the board and the three marks silently for five minutes, then talked.

### On A

:::annotate[Critique of mark A]
![Mark A: a thick rounded arch with a single leaf inside, in moss green](assets/mark-a-gate-leaf.webp)

- {#1 pin x=50% y=23.8% by=lena} The arch reads as a doorway before it reads as a logo. Good.
- {#2 pin x=52.1% y=59.8% resolved by=lena} With the leaf sitting where it does, the whole thing can read as a lowercase "n" from a distance. Check it on the sign before presenting.
- {#3 pin x=70% y=67.9% by=lena} The stroke is heavy enough to survive being painted by hand.
:::

### On B

:::annotate[Critique of mark B]
![Mark B: a thin circle holding a single fern frond, in moss green](assets/mark-b-fern-seal.webp)

- {#4 region x=35.7% y=22.9% w=29.5% h=54.5% by=lena} The fern is lovely but has about thirty edges. At the 12 mm size on a tin lid it will print as a smudge.
- {#5 pin x=18.9% y=50% by=lena} It is the one the other tea shops on the high street would pick. Nothing wrong with it, nothing particular to Mossgate either.
:::

### On C

:::annotate[Critique of mark C]
![Mark C: two overlapping leaf shapes, one moss green and one terracotta, leaving an arched gap](assets/mark-c-two-leaves.webp)

- {#6 pin x=50.4% y=53.6% by=lena} The overlap is clever, and it also looks like a flame or a tulip. The door only appears if you are told.
- {#7 pin x=62.5% y=46.8% by=lena} Two print passes on every label for a shop with a first-year budget.
:::

## Decision

Present all three, **lead with A**, and say plainly that I recommend it. Keep B as the secondary stamp (the round seal can sit on lids and bags without competing with the door) and let C go.

Open items before the presentation: test A at sign distance for comment 2, and redraw the leaf so it is clearly a leaf and not a stroke.
