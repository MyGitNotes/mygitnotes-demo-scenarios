# Chapter 3: The winter timetable

In January the *Marigold* sails four times a day instead of nine. On the third Thursday, the 2:15 carried one passenger, a postal worker named Teodor Brandt, who read a paperback the whole way.

The county's accounts show the winter service losing about $900 per crossing. The summer service nearly covers itself. Averaged over the year the ferry costs the county roughly $410,000, and everyone in the council chamber knows the number.

Those who want a bridge start here. A bridge costs far more to build, but once built it costs almost nothing to run, and it does not stop for weather.

Those who want the ferry reply that "almost nothing" has never been true of anything with concrete in it.

I do not think either side is being unreasonable. The disagreement is not about the numbers. It is about whether an island should have to ask for a timetable.
