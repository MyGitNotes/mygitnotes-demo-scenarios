# Chapter 2: The captain's ledger

Captain Idris Whitlock keeps his records in green cloth books, one per year, forty-one of them on a shelf above the wheelhouse stove.

"I write down who, what, and whether it was rough," he said. "The rest I never needed."

I asked to borrow 1998. The entry for 14 February reads: *Full. Two coffins. Fog in the middle. Late by nine minutes.* On 3 March: *Empty but for the postman.*

Read side by side, the ledger and the island's census tell the same story. In the ten years after the cannery closed, passenger numbers fell by a third. Then they climbed back, because people who worked on the mainland began to *live* on the island.

That is the ledger's quiet argument. The ferry is not carrying a shrinking place. It is carrying a place that changed what it is for.

The captain will not argue it. He closes the book and checks the tide.
