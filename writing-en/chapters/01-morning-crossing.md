# Chapter 1: The morning crossing

At 6:40 on a grey Tuesday, Dolores Ainsley was already on the dock with a bag of bread rolls that would be on the mainland by ten past seven and sold by eight. She had made the crossing, she told me, "about eleven thousand times".

The *Marigold* takes twenty-two minutes. In that time it carries a bakery delivery, three nurses, a boy with a violin case, and a man who fixes refrigerators and refuses to say which ones.

Nobody on board thinks of it as a voyage. It is a bus that happens to float.

That is the first thing an outsider gets wrong. The ferry is not a charming relic of the island. It is the island's road.

I stood at the rail and counted. Fourteen people, two vans, a pallet of eggs. If the *Marigold* did not sail, none of them would arrive, and none of them could say what they would do instead.

The next chapter asks how long this has been true, and what the captain's own figures show.
